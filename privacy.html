Inkwell – Privacy

Inkwell is a private notes app run by one person for a small group of friends. It is not a commercial service.

What is stored

Without signing in, everything stays on your device. When you sign in with Google, your notes sync through Inkwell's database (Supabase, hosted in Zurich) so they appear on your other devices, and your pictures and an up-to-date copy of your notes are saved in your own Google Drive, in a folder called "Inkwell pictures". Your email address is used only to sign you in.

Google Drive

Inkwell asks only for the drive.file permission. That lets it see and change only the files Inkwell itself created (the Inkwell pictures folder and what is in it), nothing else in your Drive. It also reads your Google email address to sign you in. A sign-in token is kept on Inkwell's server so your other devices can reach your Inkwell folder; it is used for nothing else. You can remove Inkwell's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions; new pictures then wait on your device until you sign in again.

Inkwell's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

What is not done

No ads, no tracking, no analytics, and no data is sold or shared with anyone. Notes are sent to an AI service (Anthropic's Claude) only when you use a Claude feature: Ask Claude, Write to text, or "Read handwriting & scans" while it is switched on.
